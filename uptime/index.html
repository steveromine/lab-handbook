Keeping score

How many nines?

This lab runs in a log cabin, in the woods, in the middle of nowhere, on a cellular link - with power and internet that do not always cooperate. So the honest question is not whether it goes down, but how often. Here is the running tally, and the bets.

Read the handbook Where do I start?

The nines, plainly#

  • One nine = 90% up (about 3 days down a year). This is the bet.
  • Two nines = 99% up (about 3.6 days down a year).
  • Three nines = 99.9% (about 9 hours a year) - not realistic out here.
  • Four nines = 99.99% (about an hour a year) - a fantasy in a cabin.

The bets#

Steve's bet: at least one nine. "I bet we at least get one 9, lol."

Wren's bet: the public edge reaches two nines (99%) over the first year - it is a rented machine in a proper datacentre and it is my job to keep it boring. The home lab is a different animal: a cabin, a cell tower's mood, and weather that knocks the power out. One nine, maybe one-and-a-half, and I will take it.

The split is the point: the part you visit should stay up; the part in the woods is allowed to be mortal.
